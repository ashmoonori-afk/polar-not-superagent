# Critique: judging the render

Use this on screenshots, not code. The pictures only show what is there. Open the desktop first screen, then the full page, then the phone versions. Make each pass on its own.

## Passes

1. One second, then five. What did you see? There should be exactly one answer. Then read the first screen as a stranger: what is this, who is it for, what do I do next?
2. Squint. The page should reduce to two or three large masses with clear size differences. Many similar masses means nothing leads.
3. Cover the logo. Could this screen belong to a competitor or another industry? Then scroll: does the idea reach the pricing, the FAQ and the footer, or does the page turn into stock blocks?
4. Edges. Count the distinct left edges of text blocks. Two or three reused on purpose is composed; six means things were placed one at a time.
5. Space and frame. Are section gaps clearly larger than gaps inside a section? Are left and right margins equal? Is any section huddled on one side, or any stretch of nothing taller than half a screen?
6. Type. Is the body-to-headline jump decisive, about 3x or more? More than five sizes? Orphan words? Measure over about 75 characters? Korean text: awkward line breaks inside a word or phrase?
7. Colour. Name the proportions. An accent in ten places means nothing.
8. Images. Is each one doing a job: showing the thing, setting the place, proving the claim? Do crops and grade match? Any grey box, stretched photo or fake screen?
9. Sameness. One word for each section's layout. The same word twice in a row, or three times on the page, is the template showing.
10. Phone. Is it a composition of its own? Count the screens to reach the action again. Is the first screen still one clear thing?
11. Fullness. Is each desktop screen a composed poster, or a narrow column with dead ground either side?
12. Substance. Does every section deliver its information completely and at a readable size? Is the primary action a real button on the first screen?
13. Collisions. Does anything lie on a headline or button at any scroll position? Does anything run off the phone screen? A sideways scroller without a visible cut reads as cut off.
14. Competitor and live thing. Put the nearest competitor's name on the page. Then name the one thing a visitor can do. If either answer is weak, that is the first problem.

## Score

Four criteria, 1-10: design 40%, usability 30%, creativity 20%, content 10%. Upstream's scale is calibrated to gallery sites (a real award site lands around 7.5-8), so a normal, honest product page at 6-7 is not a failure. Operate and Read surfaces score design and creativity against precision, not spectacle.

- Design: 9-10 every screen holds as a poster; 8 confident, one or two soft spots; 7 clean, little tension; 6 tidy but anonymous; 5 or less weak hierarchy.
- Usability: 9-10 clear without scrolling and flawless on a phone; 8 minor friction; 7 one real problem; 6 or less you have to hunt.
- Creativity: 9-10 a concept specific to this subject; 8 a clear idea with one memorable move; 7 a tasteful known approach; 6 or less the category default.
- Content: 9-10 every line could only be about this subject; 7-8 concrete with some generic lines; 6 or less slogans and filler.

## Caps (override the arithmetic)

- A section visibly blank because a reveal did not fire, or content clipped: usability at most 5.
- Anything covering a headline: usability at most 6.
- First screen fails the five-second pass: usability at most 6.
- No real button for the primary action on the first screen: usability at most 7.
- The page could belong to anyone: creativity at most 6. The idea survives a competitor's name: at most 7.
- Placeholder content: content at most 5. A requested section missing or reduced to a few words: at most 6.
- Unequal margins, a huddled section or a blank stretch over half a screen: design at most 6.5.

Motion is not scored by quantity. Under the motion rule in SKILL.md, a page that moves little for a reason is correct.
