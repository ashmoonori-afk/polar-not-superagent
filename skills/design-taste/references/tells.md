# Tells

A tell is a device that reads as generated because it is produced by default, everywhere, without a reason. Most began as good ideas. This list never names a replacement font or colour, because each published "use this instead" becomes the next tell. Replacements are procedures: derive from the subject.

Applies fully to Persuade and Experience surfaces. A brand asset, a pinned brief or `DESIGN.md` overrides it; note the exception. Real code, tables, form fields and real interface fragments keep their own conventions.

## First wave
- Violet or indigo gradients on hero, buttons and glows
- Gradient-filled headline text
- Glass panels, backdrop blur, translucent cards
- Glowing or gradient primary button
- Soft grey shadow under every card
- Three identical feature cards with an icon in a rounded square
- Emoji as icons or bullets
- Centred headline, subtitle, two buttons, screenshot below

## Second wave
- Eyebrow: tiny tracked uppercase label above every heading
- Pill badge above the hero headline ("New", "Beta", "Introducing")
- One word of the headline in italic serif, colour or underline
- Bento grid of rounded tiles as the feature section
- Near-black ground with one neon accent and a glow
- Floating rounded pill navigation with blur
- Fake dashboard built from grey bars and donuts
- Decorative pulsing status dot
- Fade-and-rise on every section

## Third wave: the fix that became the look
- Yellow-cream ground with terracotta, rust or clay accent
- Serif display as the shortcut to "premium"
- Broadsheet cosplay: hairlines everywhere, zero radius, dense columns
- Performative labels such as "Field notes" or "Quietly trusted by"

## Gallery fashion, house ban
Upstream's owner bans these and admits they are common on award sites. Treat them as taste findings, not defects:
- Monospace micro-labels outside real code
- Index numbering: 01 / 02 / 03, "Step 02"
- Hairline dividers, ruled grids, 1px outlined boxes
- Design-tool chrome as ornament: crosshairs, crop marks, dashed guides

## Structure
- A stock section order with no argument behind it
- A distinctive first screen followed by stock blocks
- Rows of identical cards; neighbouring sections with the same layout
- A soft 9-16 px radius on everything
- Empty space left by a removed decoration

## Copy
- A headline that names a topic instead of saying the takeaway
- Three-beat slogans, vague hooks, poetic metaphor
- Invented numbers, logos or testimonials
- A CTA named for nothing: "Learn more", "Get started", when it could say what happens

## Motion
- The same fade on every block, or content hidden until a script fires
- Motion with no cause, or scroll effects on task pages (see ui-motion rules)

## Why removal is not enough
In upstream's blind tests a richer page with tells beat a clean thin page every time. That figure is an upstream claim we have not reproduced, but the direction matches experience: fill the space with scale, full content, a real picture or a field of colour.
